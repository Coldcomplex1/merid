## Which one should you install?

It depends on what language you read the web in. English, learning something
else, Toucan. Vietnamese, learning English, Merid. That is not a diplomatic
non-answer. The two extensions genuinely run in opposite directions.

Toucan takes the page in front of you and swaps a few words into the language you
are studying. Merid takes a Vietnamese page and swaps a few Vietnamese words for
English ones. Same original idea, mirrored, and each mirror serves a different
reader.

## The comparison table

| | Toucan | Merid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Built for | People who browse in English | Vietnamese speakers browsing in Vietnamese |
| Swap direction | Into your target language | Vietnamese into English |
| Languages | Around twelve | One, English |
| Word lists | Toucan's own levels | SAT, C1, C2, or your own |
| Context checking | See their description | Yes, optional, model-backed |
| Where swapping runs | See their description | On your own machine |
| Review deck | Yes | Yes, plus puzzle and flashcard modes |
| Price | Free tier plus paid plans | Free |
| Behind it | Babbel | One student in Ho Chi Minh City |

That last row is the most honest line in the table. Toucan has a company, a team
and revenue. Merid has me.

## Where Toucan is straightforwardly better

Language coverage, maturity, and the likelihood of still existing next year.
Toucan handles roughly twelve languages. Merid does exactly one pair, Vietnamese
to English, and will not be adding a second any time soon.

Toucan has also been through years of releases and now has Babbel behind it. If
you want something stable with someone accountable when it breaks, that is a real
advantage and not a courtesy.

I am being clear about this because I would rather you install the right thing
than install mine and be disappointed. If you are learning Japanese, use Toucan.

## Where Merid wins, for this specific reader

On the only thing that decides it, which is whether the extension runs on the
pages you actually open. That is not a feature, it is a precondition. An
extension that does nothing on VnExpress or Vietnamese Facebook has no features
at all, as far as that reader is concerned.

![A Vietnamese news article with the phrase vẻ đẹp replaced by pulchritude, Merid settings panel open](/blog/screenshots/vnexpress-pulchritude.png)

The word lists differ too. Merid ships SAT, C1 and C2, which are the three targets
the people around me are aiming at. You can also build your own list if you are
studying for something specific.

Then there is the piece I spent longest on: context checking. A dictionary gives
you synonyms, but a dictionary synonym is not automatically a valid substitution
in a particular sentence. Merid can ask a model whether the word it just inserted
actually fits, and correct itself when it does not.

![A Facebook post about Messi and Ronaldo where the Merid card shows the context check replacing amorous with captivate](/blog/screenshots/facebook-captivate.png)

In that screenshot it had used *amorous*, decided that was wrong for a sentence
about two footballers, and swapped in ==captivate==.
Both map to the same Vietnamese phrase. Only one of them belongs in that sentence.

## How aggressive should the swapping be?

Gentle, at least for the first week. Merid has three intensity levels and the
lowest one is deliberately very low. If a page comes back with thirty words
changed you have stopped reading it and started decoding it, and whatever you
opened the article for is gone.

![The Merid settings panel beside a Facebook page, showing intensity levels and display modes](/blog/screenshots/sidepanel-facebook.png)

There are also three display modes: replace the word outright, only highlight it,
or place the English beside the Vietnamese. The side-by-side mode is the one to
use when you need to be certain you understood something and do not want to
guess.

## What happens to the words you meet?

They go to a deck, if you save them. Mark one as known and Merid stops swapping
it in. Here is my deck at the time I took the screenshot: twenty-five words,
fifteen in progress, ten known.

![The Merid deck page listing 25 words including hardy, innovate, copious and amalgamate](/blog/screenshots/my-deck.png)

It contains *hardy*, *innovate*, *copious*, *amalgamate* and *confluence*. I did
not sit down and study a single one of them. They turned up while I was reading
things I was going to read anyway, and I kept them.

That is the entire idea, and it is why I abandoned every vocabulary app I
installed before this. They all wanted their own slot in my day. This one does
not ask for one.
