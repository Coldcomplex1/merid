in the dropdown, the answer is in the block above. What follows is the part that

instead of waiting for an update.

## Is Vietnamese on Toucan's language list?

It is not. Toucan's published list covers Spanish, French, German, Italian,
Portuguese, Japanese, Korean, Hindi, Hebrew, Arabic, Chinese and English.
Vietnamese does not appear on it.

Toucan is owned by Babbel now, so the authoritative place to check is the Babbel
help centre or the extension's own Chrome Web Store description. Any list in a
blog post, including this one, goes stale the moment a company ships a new
language.

## Why is the language list the smaller problem?

Because Toucan translates in one direction, and adding a language name would not
change that direction. Toucan assumes the page in front of you is written in a
language you already read comfortably, and it swaps a handful of those words into
the language you are studying.

For Toucan's core user that means an English-language page and a Spanish word
appearing in it. You read a news article, *bread* comes out as *pan*, and one
word lands.

Now invert every part of that. The pages a Vietnamese reader opens all day are
VnExpress, Tuổi Trẻ, Vietnamese Wikipedia, Vietnamese Facebook. The language they
want is English. So the swap has to run from Vietnamese into English, on a
Vietnamese page.

That is not one missing menu item. It is a different product.

## Can I just set English as the language to learn?

You can select it, but it will not do what you want. English is on Toucan's list
of languages you can learn. The catch is that the page you are reading has to be
in a language Toucan handles as your base, and Vietnamese is not one of them.

Which means the only way to make Toucan swap words for you is to already be
reading the web in English. If you can comfortably read a full English page, your
vocabulary problem has moved on to something else.

I mention this because several Vietnamese-language write-ups about Toucan skip
it. They list the features, praise the idea, and leave the reader to discover
after installing that there is nothing for them to select. The idea genuinely is
good. It was just not built for this reader.

## So what should a Vietnamese learner use instead?

Something that runs Vietnamese to English. It has to read a Vietnamese page,
choose a few words worth replacing, and drop an English word in their place
without wrecking the sentence.

That is what I built, and I built it because I could not find one. I am a student
in Ho Chi Minh City, and I quit every vocabulary app I ever downloaded. Not
because they were bad. Because all of them needed me to open them first.

## How is Merid actually different?

Three ways: the direction of the swap, the word lists, and where the work
happens. Toucan serves English speakers learning something else. Merid serves
Vietnamese speakers learning English. Everything else follows from that.

| | Toucan | Merid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Swap direction | Page language into your target language | Vietnamese into English |
| Pages it works on | Pages in a language Toucan supports | Vietnamese pages you read anyway |
| Word lists | Toucan's own levels | SAT, C1, C2, or a list you build |
| Where it runs | Per Toucan's description | Word replacement happens on your machine |
| Price | Free tier plus paid plans | Free |

Merid ships three lists, SAT, C1 and C2. I picked those three because they are
the three targets the people around me are actually aiming at, not because they
were the easiest to assemble.

The part I am most attached to is the context check. Merid can ask a model
whether the word it just swapped in genuinely fits the sentence it landed in.

## Is it worth waiting for Toucan to add Vietnamese?

If you are learning Spanish or Japanese and you browse in English, do not wait
for anything. Toucan works today and does its job well. This post is not an
argument that Toucan is bad.

If you are Vietnamese and you want more English vocabulary while reading
Vietnamese news, waiting for a name to appear in a dropdown will not help. The
thing that needs to change is not the list, it is the direction. That has to be
built from scratch, which is the part I already did.
